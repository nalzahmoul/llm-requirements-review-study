# Software Requirements Specification: Cool Ski Resorts Online Reservation & Management System

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Purpose
This document states the software requirements for Cool Ski Resorts' new online system, based on the elicitation interview. Each requirement shows its source and confidence:

- **[S] Stated:** the stakeholder said it explicitly.
- **[C] Confirmed:** the stakeholder agreed to something the interviewer proposed. This is lower confidence, because some confirmations were one-word answers to compound questions.
- **[D] Derived:** inferred from context. It needs stakeholder validation.

### 1.2 Business Context
Cool Ski Resorts runs three newly opened ski resorts in different US mountain locations. Each resort offers three services:
- Hotel room bookings
- Ski equipment and clothing hire
- Ski training classes

The current process has five main weaknesses:
- Reservations and hires are taken by telephone and fax.
- Payments are handled semi-manually at each resort.
- Data is held in a separate local database at each resort.
- Coordination between the resorts is becoming difficult.
- The website is static and shows only service information and contact details.

### 1.3 Business Objectives
| ID | Objective |
|----|-----------|
| BO-1 | Increase visibility and attract customers nationwide. The resorts are new and currently have very few customers. |
| BO-2 | Increase revenue through online reservation, booking, and payment. |
| BO-3 | Streamline operations across the three resorts through a centralized database. |
| BO-4 | Make business reporting easier. |
| BO-5 | Gain a competitive edge through innovative digital features. |
| BO-6 | Restructure the business digitally with minimal disruption to current operations. |

### 1.4 Scope
**In scope:**
- Public website
- Online reservations, hires, and class bookings
- Online payment
- Centralized database
- Inventory management
- Reporting
- Weather and snow-condition display
- Social media integration

**Out of scope (as stated):**
- Internal tools for coordinating social media content creation or team communication

## 6. Assumptions

- **A-1:** "US weather forecast system" means the National Weather Service (NOAA) public API.
- **A-2:** "Post about jews from the resort" is a typo for "**news** from the resort."
- **A-3:** Telephone bookings will continue alongside online bookings.
- **A-4:** Each resort keeps local staff who will use the system for daily operations.
- **A-5:** "Mary/Jim Smith" is one owner. The name was given ambiguously.

## 7. Open Issues, Conflicts, and Risks

| ID | Issue | Impact | Recommended Action |
|----|-------|--------|-------------------|
| **OI-1** | **Conflict: synchronization versus online booking.** The stakeholder wants infrequent scheduled syncs, but live online booking needs current availability. If resort-side bookings sync only occasionally, the same room, item, or class place could be sold twice. | High: double-bookings and customer dissatisfaction | Clarify the architecture. Either make all bookings, including phone bookings, go directly through the central system in real time, or limit scheduled sync to non-critical data such as reports and historical records. Define the sync frequency. |
| OI-2 | **Snow-condition data source.** The NWS API provides forecasts but may not provide on-mountain snow conditions such as base depth or slope status. | Medium | Confirm data needs. Consider NOAA NOHRSC data, resort-entered snow reports, or a specialist ski-conditions provider. |
| OI-3 | **Low-confidence social media requirements.** "Yes" was given to a compound question covering auto-sharing, user-generated content, and reviews. It is also unclear whether weekly posts are published through the system or directly on each platform. | Medium | Confirm each feature separately and decide whether automated cross-posting is needed. |
| OI-4 | **Inventory scope undefined.** It is unclear whether inventory covers only hire items or also hotel supplies and retail. Stock alerts, maintenance tracking, and procurement were not discussed. | Medium | Run a follow-up session with inventory staff. |
| OI-5 | **Pain points not elicited.** "It sucks" gave no actionable detail about the current system. | Medium | Interview resort staff (accounts, customer service, inventory) directly. |
| OI-6 | **Undiscussed areas.** No input was gathered on customer accounts and login, cancellation and refund policy, pricing and promotions or discount codes, package deals (room plus hire plus class), languages, accessibility, budget, timeline, or hosting. | Medium–High | Hold a follow-up elicitation session. |
| OI-7 | **"Innovative solutions."** The stakeholder wants competitive innovation but named no specific features beyond weather integration. | Low–Medium | Propose options such as package bundles, loyalty programs, and dynamic pricing for stakeholder review. |
| OI-8 | **Accounting and tax integration.** It is unclear whether the system should integrate with existing accounting software or produce tax-ready reports. | Medium | Consult the accounts assistants. |
| OI-9 | **Reporting format.** Report frequency, format (dashboard or export), and recipients were not specified. | Low | Confirm with the owner. |

## 8. Reviewer Notes on the Elicitation

The interview captured high-level features well, but the stakeholder's answers were often brief or single-word. Several requirements rest on interviewer suggestions rather than stakeholder statements. The main concerns are:

1. **The architecture conflict in OI-1** is the most significant risk and should be resolved before design begins.
2. **No operational staff were interviewed.** Accounts, customer service, and inventory staff are key users, so further sessions with them are strongly recommended.
3. **Requirements marked [C] and [D]** should be validated with the stakeholder before the document is baselined.
